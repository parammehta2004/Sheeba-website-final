## HOW TO READ THIS DOCUMENT

Each concept is built from a different **governing question** — the one question the homepage is trying to answer for the visitor.

- **Concept 1** answers: *"Why is Sheeba different from every expert I've already seen?"*
- **Concept 2** answers: *"What would my life feel like if my body finally worked with me?"*
- **Concept 3** answers: *"Is this the moment I stop accepting how I feel?"*

All three use the same content, the same palette, the same typefaces. What differs is the *narrative stance*, the *entry point into the brand world*, and the *emotional journey* the visitor takes through the page.

# CONCEPT 1 — "THE CARTOGRAPHER"

> *The world of the expert who reads what others cannot.*

---

## NARRATIVE

The visitor arrives as a sceptic — or as someone who has already seen multiple specialists and not found answers. The page leads with Sheeba's singular capability: she reads complexity that others flatten into generic conclusions.

The narrative arc is: **Recognition → Evidence → Method → Invitation**

1. "You have been seen incompletely. I see the whole picture."
2. "This is what my reading has accomplished for real people, in measurable terms."
3. "Here is how I do it — precisely and systematically."
4. "Here is how you begin."

The tone is confident without being cold. It mirrors the profile of a world-class consultant who does not need to oversell because the work speaks with complete authority.

This concept targets **Persona B** (the health-conscious professional, the sceptic) while still retaining emotional resonance for Persona A. The Magician archetype is most prominent here.

---

## VISUAL METAPHOR

**The Map Room.** 

A 17th-century cartographer's study transposed into the present. The walls hold maps — but these are maps of the body: blood chemistry charts, mineral grids, bio-field diagrams, botanical specimen records. Every map is an individual; every individual is a map that has never been drawn before.

The visual language is: **precise line on warm paper.**

Draft lines. Cross-hatching. The aesthetic of the scientific illustration — where accuracy and beauty are not opposites but the same thing. Every diagram is rendered in the brand palette: sage ink on alabaster parchment, amber for emphasis, olive for depth.

The world is indoor but not enclosed — light comes from a large window that we never fully see. The light falls across the surface of things: the paper, the desk, the leaf pressing between journal pages.

---

## BRAND WORLD

The brand world here is the **intersection of the research library and the private consultation.** It is formal enough to signal expertise, warm enough to signal care. It is the world of the person who has read everything — and therefore knows what the textbooks miss.

Textures: warm parchment, pressed botanical specimens, manuscript ink, morning window light, linen, aged wood.

Ambience: quiet, focused, unhurried. The world of someone who gives a full hour of complete attention to a single problem.

---

## HERO CONCEPT

**Full-viewport split: Left is Sheeba, Right is the Map.**

**Left panel (60% width):**  
A large, editorial portrait of Sheeba — not a headshot, not a full-body pose. A three-quarter portrait, natural light from the left, in olive-green. Expression: direct, calm, certain. No smile required — the warmth comes from the eyes, not the mouth.

Behind her, slightly out of focus: the shelves of a working library. Not staged. Not perfect.

**Right panel (40% width):**  
Not a photograph. A living diagram — the kind Sheeba actually draws. A blood chemistry map, rendered in the brand palette. Animated: not all at once, but as if being drawn in real time. Lines appear slowly, labels emerge, dots connect. The diagram resolves into something beautiful and precise.

This is the visual thesis of the entire concept: **Sheeba on the left, the map of the body on the right. Together they say: this person can read what doctors cannot.**

**Headline (over the left panel, set in Playfair Display):**
```
"Your blood test told a story.
Everyone else missed it."
```

*"Your"* and *"missed"* are in italic. The rest is regular weight.

**Subheading (Rubik, 18px, below):**
```
Sheeba Majmudar — Naturopathic Nutritionist, Functional Medicine Practitioner,
Nutritionist of the Year 2020 — reads what your doctors overlook.
```

**CTA (single, olive pill button):**
```
Begin your assessment →
```

No second CTA. No secondary link. One action.

## SECTION STRUCTURE

### Section 1 — The Hero
*(Described above)*

### Section 2 — "The Pattern She Sees" (what sets her apart)
**Concept:** A horizontal scrolling strip or slow-reveal sequence of three "case observations."

Not testimonials (those come later). These are Sheeba's *observations about patterns she has noticed* that doctors miss — written in her voice, in the first person. Brief. Precise.

Examples of copy direction:
- *"Most fatigue cases I see have optimal iron — but suboptimal ferritin. The standard test doesn't distinguish. I do."*
- *"Blood sugar issues and insomnia are the same conversation. Most practitioners treat them separately. I don't."*
- *"Hormonal imbalance rarely begins with hormones. It begins with gut dysbiosis, then liver detoxification, then hormones. You have to start at the beginning."*

Visual: Each observation appears as a hand-annotated "chart" — a simple diagram with Sheeba's marginal notes, rendered in sage ink.

### Section 3 — The Credentials (rendered as a map legend)
**Concept:** The 10 credentials, 5 awards, and press features — presented not as a badge wall but as a **map legend.** In cartography, the legend tells you how to read the map. Here, it tells you how to read Sheeba.

Layout: A single column of credential types (Education · Practice · Awards · Media), each with its items set in small caps. The press logos (BBC, CNA, Straits Times) appear as a small, restrained row — not the "AS SEEN IN" banner of typical sites.

The section header is a single line:  
*"Her credentials are why doctors refer to her."*

### Section 4 — Evidence: The Outcomes Map
**Concept:** The 12 condition groups visualised as a **radial map** — a circular diagram where each condition is a point on the circumference, and clicking any point reveals the associated testimonials.

On desktop: the full radial diagram. Interactive — hover a condition, the testimonials for that condition appear in a side panel.

On mobile: accordion by condition category.

The visual here is pure to the "cartographer" concept: the map of conditions is literally a map, and the client's name + age + outcome is the territory it represents.

### Section 5 — The Method ("How I work")
The 4-step process from the original site (Assessment → Consultation → Protocol → Follow-up) — rendered as a **journey line.** Not a numbered grid. A single horizontal line with four points, each point a small circle with a label. Between points, the line is drawn in sage. The overall shape feels like a trail on a topographic map.

### Section 6 — The Book
"Edible to Incredible" — presented not as a product promotion but as evidence of intellectual depth. The book cover, a single pull quote from the book, and the Amazon link. Restrained, editorial, brief.

### Section 7 — The Enquiry (the map ends here)
**Concept:** The form is presented as the first step of the map — the starting point before the journey begins. Header: *"Every map begins with one question: Where are you now?"*

The form fields are labelled in Sheeba's voice, not standard form labels:
- "Your name" (not "First Name / Last Name")
- "How can I reach you?" (not "Phone / Email")
- "Tell me what's been going on" (not "Remarks")

Submit button: *"Start the conversation →"*

---

## IMAGERY DIRECTION

- Sheeba: editorial portrait, three-quarter, natural light, olive green, direct gaze
- Diagrams: hand-drawn aesthetic, sage ink on cream, animated line-draw
- Botanical elements: microscopic precision — cross-sections, vascular detail
- No lifestyle photography. No food photography. No stock imagery.
- The visual world is: **the practitioner's desk, the specimen case, the map room window.**

---

## MOTION LANGUAGE

**Primary motion: The Draw**

Everything important in this concept is drawn into existence — not faded, not slid. The hero diagram draws itself. The 4-step journey line draws from left to right as the user scrolls. The radial map draws its spokes outward from the centre.

The draw motion communicates: *precision taking shape in real time. Knowledge becoming visible.*

- Drawing speed: slow (800ms–1400ms per element)
- Easing: linear start, ease-out finish — as a real pen would decelerate
- Stagger: each element of the diagram draws 200ms after the previous

**Secondary motion: The Reveal**
Text reveals: opacity + slight translate-up, slow (1.0s). Nothing snappy. The page breathes.

**Ambient:** The Lottie plant in the lower-right, as always. Here it feels like a live specimen in the map room — the only thing that breathes in an otherwise still world.

# CONCEPT 2 — "THE GARDEN AT DAWN"

> *The world where nature is the most sophisticated scientist.*

---

## NARRATIVE

The visitor arrives not as a sceptic, but as someone who already believes there must be a better way — they just haven't found the person who can lead them to it. This concept does not argue for Sheeba's credentials. It *shows* the world she lives in, and trusts that the right visitor will recognise it.

The narrative arc is: **Recognition of a world → Desire to enter it → Understanding what lives there → The invitation to step inside**

1. "There is a world where your body is not broken — only misread."
2. "Nature has been solving these problems for thousands of years."
3. "Sheeba speaks both languages: the language of the lab and the language of the garden."
4. "This is the door. It is open."

The tone is unhurried, almost meditative. The Healer archetype leads. This concept speaks most directly to **Persona A** (the frustrated seeker who has tried everything and wants something that feels *different* before she can evaluate it intellectually).

---

## VISUAL METAPHOR

**The Garden at Dawn.**

Specifically: the moment before the world fully wakes — when the light is golden-green and the air is still and you can hear individual sounds because there is no background noise yet. This is the moment the body can hear itself.

The visual language is: **organic depth and morning warmth.**

Depth of field. Foreground sharp, background luminous and soft. Natural textures — bark, leaf surface, root structure, morning dew. The colour palette at its most saturated (still warm, still the brand palette — but with more amber, more gold, more green in the light).

The world is outdoor but not exposed — sheltered by a canopy of something living. There is always something growing at the edges of the frame.

---

## BRAND WORLD

The brand world here is **the apothecary garden of someone who also holds an MSc.** 

It is the world of slow, deep knowledge — where a plant growing in specific soil produces a specific compound that addresses a specific cellular imbalance. Where the body is not broken; it is *in conversation with its environment*, and the conversation has been disrupted.

Textures: living wood, wet stone, morning light through leaf canopy, the inside of a fig, the root ball of a pulled herb, warm amber glass (vials, tinctures), linen.

Ambience: soft, still, full of specific sounds if you listen — but the site itself is silent except for the motion of things growing.

---

## HERO CONCEPT

**Full-viewport immersive: A single image that takes the entire screen.**

Not Sheeba. Not a diagram. A photograph.

The photograph: morning light through a canopy of leaves — the kind of image that makes you feel the temperature of the air before you see a single person. Warm green, amber light, the specific beauty of a world that was here before medicine was invented and will be here after it.

Over this image, centred, set in Playfair Display:

**Headline:**
```
The body already knows
how to heal.

It just needs to be
listened to.
```

Each line appears individually, unhurried. The first two lines in regular weight. The second pair in italic.

Below the headline, a single line in Rubik, 16px, slightly translucent white:
```
Sheeba Majmudar · Naturopathic Nutritionist · Nutritionist of the Year 2020
```

**CTA:** A single button, but it does not say "Submit" or "Book" or "Learn More."

It says:
```
Come in  →
```

The invitation is to *enter* — not to transact.

Below the hero, a gentle downward scroll indicator: a single leaf, slowly drifting down. Not an arrow. A leaf.

## SECTION STRUCTURE

### Section 1 — The Hero
*(Described above)*

### Section 2 — "The Garden's Intelligence" (the philosophy)
**Concept:** A wide, unhurried text section — generous margins, generous line-height, generous vertical space. No columns. No cards. Just words.

A single, carefully written statement of Sheeba's philosophy — not a service description, not a credentials list. A statement of *belief*:

> *"I have spent decades studying the body's conversation with itself — the signals it sends when something is wrong, the language it speaks in blood markers and mineral ratios and energy patterns. Conventional medicine reads the same language but hears only half the conversation. I hear all of it."*

Below this: a slow, ambient botanical illustration — not as a separate section, but bleeding into the text from the right margin. A single stem with three leaves, drawn in sage. It grows as the user scrolls.

### Section 3 — Sheeba (the person, not the award-winner)
**Concept:** An intimate portrait section — not the credential-forward About section. This is the *person*.

A large, warm photograph of Sheeba — this time in her own environment, not posed. Looking at something, not at the camera. The image is wide, editorial, the light is the same morning warmth as the hero.

Below the image, two short paragraphs in Rubik 18px:

First paragraph: who she is (Canadian-born, lived globally, the east-west synthesis of her practice).

Second paragraph: why she does this (her own healing journey, Biodynamic Craniosacral, the moment she understood that the body has its own intelligence).

No awards listed here. No credentials. Those come later, for those who need them. This section is for *recognition* — the visitor recognising a person who thinks the way they do.

### Section 4 — Outcomes from the Garden
**Concept:** The testimonials rendered as *field notes.* 

Each testimonial group (Diabetes, Hormonal, Weight Loss, etc.) is treated as a category in a botanical field study. The condition name appears in small, sage-coloured uppercase. Below it: the testimonial, in Playfair Italic, large enough to feel like a quote, not a paragraph.

The layout: alternating columns, wide margins, plenty of white space. Two testimonials visible at a time. A slow carousel to the next pair.

The visual texture behind each quote: a subtle pressed-leaf pattern at very low opacity — as if the testimonial is itself a specimen.

### Section 5 — The World She Works With (services)
**Concept:** Not a service grid. A **garden map** — a visual layout of the different "territories" of her practice.

Divided into two areas, rendered as organic shapes rather than columns:
- **The Assessment Garden** (Functional Blood Chemistry, Dutch Test, Hair Mineral Analysis, Compatibility Test) — these are diagnostic: the roots of the practice
- **The Treatment Garden** (NES, Aromatherapy, Gemmotherapy, Reconnective Healing, Craniosacral, Dropzone) — these are restorative: the bloom of the practice

Each area is introduced with a line of Playfair italic. Each service appears as a seed — a name, a single sentence, a leaf icon.

### Section 6 — The Process (entering the garden)
The 4-step process — rendered as a **seasonal sequence.** 

Not steps 1-4. Seasons:
- *Winter* (Assessment) — going inward, examining what's there
- *Spring* (Consultation) — the conversation that begins the growth
- *Summer* (Protocol) — the full expression of the programme
- *Autumn* (Follow-up) — harvesting what's changed, preparing what's next

Visual: a horizontal sequence of four botanical illustrations, one per season. Beneath each: the step title and description.

### Section 7 — The Open Door (contact)
**Concept:** The form is placed inside a full-width section with the warmest, most amber-rich section background on the page — like entering the house from the garden.

Header: *"The door is always open."*

The Lottie door-animation from the original About page is moved here — it plays on entering this section, the door opening slowly, invitingly.

Form fields in Sheeba's voice (same as Concept 1). Submit: *"Walk in →"*

---

## IMAGERY DIRECTION

- Hero: dawn garden canopy — specific, photographed, warm green and amber light
- Sheeba: environmental portrait — looking at something, natural light, not posed
- Botanical details: specific plant close-ups — not romantic, but with the precision of a field study
- Section backgrounds: progressive warmth — the page gets warmer (more amber) as it moves toward the contact section
- No clinical imagery. No diagrams in this concept — that is Concept 1's territory.
- The page feels like you're moving through a garden toward a building.

---

## MOTION LANGUAGE

**Primary motion: The Grow**

Things in this concept do not appear — they *grow*. Botanical illustrations extend from points. Text sections rise slowly from below like something emerging from soil. The hero text appears letter-group by letter-group, not word by word — phrases, not telegrams.

- Growth speed: very slow (1.4s–2.0s)
- Easing: ease-in-out organic — like breathing, not mechanical
- The growing botanical illustration in Section 2 extends 2px per 1px of scroll

**Secondary motion: The Drift**
The scroll indicator is a drifting leaf. Floating elements (the "Come in" button) have a very gentle ambient float: `translateY: 0 → -8px → 0` on a 4-second loop.

**Ambient:** The plant Lottie is most at home in this concept. Here it is not peripheral — it is a participant. It might be slightly larger, or positioned with more visual intentionality, at the junction between sections.

# CONCEPT 3 — "THE TURNING POINT"

> *The world of the moment everything changed.*

---

## NARRATIVE

This concept does not lead with Sheeba at all.

It leads with the visitor.

Specifically: with the *feeling* of the visitor's life before they found the answer they were looking for. It names the experience — exhaustion, confusion, the shame of not being fixed by anything — without melodrama, without exploitation. Then it offers a turning point. Then it demonstrates what that turning point looks like in measurable, named, real human outcomes. Then it presents Sheeba as the cause of those outcomes.

The narrative arc is: **Recognition of the visitor's experience → The existence of a different possibility → Evidence of that possibility → The person who makes it possible → The first step**

This is the most emotionally direct concept. The Healer archetype leads, but the Sage closes.

The order of trust is: *emotion first, credentials second* — which is the reverse of Concept 1.

This concept speaks most powerfully to **Persona A** (the frustrated seeker) and **Persona C** (the referral arrival who needs to see her own situation reflected).

---

## VISUAL METAPHOR

**The Turning Point itself — the instant where light re-enters.**

In Japanese: *Ma* — the pregnant pause between two states. Not the before, not the after. The breath between them.

Visually: the moment a room fills with light when a door is opened. The moment a plant first breaks through soil. The moment a blood test comes back better than anyone expected.

The visual language is: **contrast and resolution.** 

The page opens in relative shadow — rich, warm darkness, the feeling of something enclosed. As the visitor scrolls, light enters. By the time they reach the testimonials, the page is luminous. By the contact form, it is at full warmth.

This is not a light/dark mode — both are warm, both are within the brand palette. But the tonal value shifts visually from deep olive-amber to bright alabaster-sage as you scroll.

---

## BRAND WORLD

The brand world here is **the private moment of transformation** — the single conversation, the single blood test reading, the single session that changed everything.

It is not a world of place (as in Concept 2) or expertise (as in Concept 1). It is a world of *time* — a specific moment that divides a life into before and after.

Textures: shadow and light, the specific quality of being heard, the texture of a letter written by hand, warm amber against dark olive, a single candle in a dark room, the moment before speech.

Ambience: intimate, still, the feeling of being in a private conversation.

---

## HERO CONCEPT

**A two-act hero — the visitor's experience, then the turning point.**

**Act 1 (The Recognition) — Upper half of viewport:**

Not an image. Not Sheeba. A single, large typographic statement in Playfair Display, centred, in deep foreground colour on the brand background:

```
"For years, you've been told
you're fine.

You don't feel fine."
```

*"You don't feel fine."* in italic, slightly larger, slightly more amber in colour.

No image. No decoration. Just the words and the weight of space around them.

**Act 2 (The Turning Point) — Lower half, revealed on a 1.5s scroll trigger:**

As the visitor scrolls a fraction, the hero transitions. The text above fades — not abruptly, but slowly, as if a cloud has passed — and a new visual takes the full viewport:

Sheeba's face. Full bleed. Not a portrait — a moment. The fraction of a second before she speaks in a consultation. Complete attention. Complete presence. The kind of look that says: *"I am going to find out what's actually wrong."*

Over this image, a single Playfair italic line:  
```
"She changed everything."
```
— attributed: *Rachel, 32. Natural pregnancy after years of infertility treatment.*

The attribution is small. The quote is large. This is the first testimonial the visitor sees — chosen because it is the most emotionally complete outcome on the entire site.

**CTA:** Below the image, a single pale olive pill button:
```
This could be your turning point  →
```

## SECTION STRUCTURE

### Section 1 — The Hero
*(Described above — the two-act typographic/portrait hero)*

### Section 2 — "You Are Not Alone in This" (the entry states)
**Concept:** A horizontal slow-reveal of the three emotional entry states (from PERSONALITY.md) — not clinical descriptions, but first-person acknowledgements.

Three panels, each revealed as the previous fades on scroll:

Panel A: *"You've seen the doctors. You've done the tests. You've been told everything looks normal. And yet."*

Panel B: *"You've been given the same diet advice, the same generic plan, the same medication everyone else gets. It hasn't worked for you."*

Panel C: *"You've started to wonder if this is just how it is now."*

These are not questions — they are statements of recognition. They do not ask "do you feel this way?" They say "this is what you feel." The difference is significant: the former is diagnostic, the latter is *understood*.

Below all three: a single line in sage, Playfair italic:  
*"It isn't how it has to be."*

Visual: each panel sits against a progressively lighter background — the page beginning its transition from shadow to light.

### Section 3 — The Outcomes (the evidence of the turning point)
**Concept:** The testimonials as the *primary content* of this concept — elevated above credentials, above services, above everything except the opening recognition.

Layout: a cinematic single-testimonial display. One testimonial fills the visual space. Full name, age, condition, and the complete verbatim quote — not excerpted, not truncated.

The condition is displayed as a small badge above the quote. The name and age below, in Rubik.

Navigation: left/right arrows, slow crossfade between testimonials. The background shifts subtly between testimonials — each has a slightly different tonal warmth within the palette, as if the light in the room changes between stories.

**The logic:** By showing complete testimonials rather than excerpts, the page communicates that these are *real people with complex stories who were seen as individuals* — not soundbite-sized proof points.

### Section 4 — "Who Made This Possible" (Sheeba)
**Concept:** Only here — after the visitor has read the testimonials — does the page introduce Sheeba formally.

A wide, generous portrait section. Sheeba in three-quarter, natural light, green. The same intimacy as Concept 2 but the framing is different: here she appears as the *answer* to the question the testimonials created ("who did this?").

Headline: *"Sheeba Majmudar. The person who changed all of this."*

Below: a short biography paragraph (not credentials first — the story first). Then a restrained credential row: five awards in small caps, the publication list, the doctor referrals. Brief. Verified. Not boastful.

CTA within this section: *"Get to know her →"* (links to About page)

### Section 5 — The Method (why it works)
**Concept:** The 4-step process — but narrated, not listed.

Not a numbered grid. A short editorial text block that describes the process *as a story*:

> *"It begins with a conversation and a blood test. From that, Sheeba draws a map — a complete picture of what your body is actually doing, not what the reference ranges say it should be doing. From the map, she builds a protocol. Not a programme. A protocol: specific to you, adjustable, monitored. And then she follows up — because the body changes, and the work changes with it."*

Below this text: the four steps as small, restrained labels. Not cards. Just the words, in Rubik Medium, numbered in sage.

### Section 6 — Services (briefly)
**Concept:** The lightest treatment of services in any of the three concepts — because in this narrative, the *outcome* precedes the *method*. By the time the visitor reaches services, they already want to begin. The services section answers: "what will this actually look like?"

Two simple lists: Assessments and Treatments. Names only. No descriptions at this stage. A single CTA beneath: *"Explore all services →"*

### Section 7 — The Enquiry (the first step)
**Concept:** The form in this concept is framed as *the turning point itself* — the moment the visitor crosses the threshold.

The section background: the warmest amber of the entire page — the moment of full light.

Headline (large, Playfair italic): *"Every turning point begins with a single step."*

Subheading: *"Tell Sheeba where you are. She'll help you figure out where to go."*

The form fields carry the same voice as the other concepts. Submit: *"Take the first step →"*

After submission: the confirmation does not say "Thank you! Your submission has been received!" It says: *"Sheeba will be in touch. This is the beginning."*

---

## IMAGERY DIRECTION

- Hero Act 1: no image — pure typography in space. The absence of image is intentional: the visitor's experience is invisible, internal. It deserves a visual language that does not impose.
- Hero Act 2: Sheeba in the moment before speech — not posed, not smiling, completely present
- Section 2: no images — text only against progressively lightening backgrounds
- Section 3 (testimonials): avatar illustration (the existing female/male SVG avatars from the original site) — human but not photographic, consistent with the "real person, protected identity" feel
- Section 4 (Sheeba): environmental portrait — the warmest, most present photograph of her on the site
- Throughout: the tonal progression from dark amber-olive → bright alabaster-sage is itself the primary visual element

---

## MOTION LANGUAGE

**Primary motion: The Emergence**

Things in this concept do not draw, and do not grow. They *emerge* — as if they were always there, and the light has found them.

- Text reveals: pure opacity, very slow (1.6–2.0s), no translate. Just the text becoming visible.
- The hero transition (Act 1 → Act 2): a 1.5-second crossfade, triggered by the first scroll gesture. Not on a timer.
- Section 2 panels: each panel fades in only when the previous has fully resolved.
- Testimonials: individual word-group fade-in — the testimonial text arrives as if being read aloud, phrase by phrase, at reading pace.

**Secondary motion: The Light Shift**
The background colour transitions continuously as the user scrolls — using a CSS custom property animated by scroll position. The page literally gets lighter as you move through it. The transition is barely perceptible in the moment but unmistakable in total.

**Ambient:** The Lottie plant, always present, but positioned differently in this concept — at the bottom-left, as if growing from the base of the page upward. Its growth direction mirrors the page's upward movement toward light.
